# Exercise 8.19 rack and pinion native coupling

The Arbor Press requires a rotary-to-linear mate. The existing Gear mate relates two angular motions and cannot express ram travel. The new canonical type is `rackpinion`. Reference A is the pinion's exact axis; reference B is the rack's directed travel axis. Both must resolve to valid axes with perpendicular directions. The frozen press can use its pinion shaft cylinder and ram bottom socket cylinder, avoiding a guessed display-mesh direction.

The ideal relation is Δtravel = signed pitch radius × Δpinion angle, with angles in radians and lengths in canonical centimetres. `pitchRadius` must be finite and positive; `reverse=true` changes the sign. The frozen pitch radius is 1 cm (10 mm). With pinion +Z and rack +Y, closing 60 mm requires Drive −6/(2π) = −0.954929658551372 revolutions. A selected native ram socket axis may point −Y; that direction is honored, so reverse/drive sign must be selected according to the actual reference. The mate is ideal kinematic coupling, not generated tooth contact, force transmission or a load-capacity calculation.

`pinionRoll0` and `rackTravel0` capture the authored home. Rotation is measured relative to the rack component frame so rigidly placing the whole press into another assembly does not turn common yaw into ram travel. Native reference axes define direction, while component-origin displacement defines travel: a socket rebuild may move its reported cylindrical centroid without moving the ram. Zero fields persist through ordinary native JSON export/reopen.

A smooth periodic sine/cosine phase supplies one independent coupling constraint; linear travel carries complete pinion revolutions across orientation wrapping. Optional finite `driveTurns` adds the requested unwrapped travel and pinion orientation, retaining arbitrary positive or negative complete turns. Blank Drive leaves the mechanism free. Bearing concentric/axial capture, the two nonparallel ram guide planes, and the signed 30–90 mm travel Limit remain separate ordinary mates. Their two initial freedoms become one with free rack coupling and zero with a driving turn. The coupling does not manufacture a guide or silently ground either component.
