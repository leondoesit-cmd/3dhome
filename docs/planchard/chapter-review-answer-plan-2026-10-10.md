## Chapter1 — PDF79 / printed1-51 — 15 questions

|Q|Short topic|Answer for worksheet|
|---:|---|---|
|1|Cartesian coordinates|Perpendicular X,Y,Z axes locate a point by signed distances from a common origin. In a2D sketch use two axes; the third describes the normal/depth direction.|
|2|Axis intersection|The origin, with coordinates(0,0,0), or(0,0)in2D.|
|3|Right-hand rule|Point the right-hand index along+X and middle finger along+Y; the thumb points+Z. Equivalently curl from+X toward+Y and the thumb gives+Z. Positive rotation curls around a positively directed axis with the thumb along that axis.|
|4|Freehand sketching|It communicates and tests shape, proportions and spatial relationships quickly, before detailed CAD or manufacturing dimensions.|
|5|First versus third angle|First angle places the object between observer and projection plane; Top is below Front and Right is left of Front. Third angle places the plane between observer and object; Top is above Front and Right is right of Front.|
|6|US first-angle statement|False under the book's US convention: the book uses third-angle projection.|
|7|Line precedence|When lines coincide, draw the higher-priority one: Visible,Hidden,Cutting-plane,Center,Phantom,Break,Dimension,Extension/Leader,Section. A visible edge replaces a coincident hidden line; a hidden line overrides a coincident centerline. Source priority list is PDF51/1-23.|
|8|Hidden overrides visible|False. A visible/object line takes priority over a coincident hidden line.|
|9|2D quadrants|True: two intersecting perpendicular axes divide the plane into quadrantsI–IV. Conventional numbering starts upper-right and proceeds counterclockwise.|
|10|Glass box|Place the part inside a transparent box, project perpendicular to each face, then unfold the faces into the chosen first-/third-angle arrangement. The aligned views show width,height,depth consistently.|
|11|Same six views|True. Front,Back,Top,Bottom,Left,Right view directions are the same; the sheet arrangement changes.|
|12|Hatch material indication|True. Section hatch conventions can distinguish material types; a material specification can still be needed for an exact grade.|
|13|Mechanical dimension endings|True within the book's stated mechanical-drawing rule: dimension lines end with arrowheads. Architectural slash/dot practice is separately identified in the source.|
|14|Break lines|True with distinction: they mark an omitted portion/imaginary break. A long shaft can be shortened on paper; a broken-out view exposes a local interior. A full section uses a cutting-plane/section-view convention.|
|15|Front selection|True as a design guideline. Choose the most informative orientation with few hidden lines; project the other views from it. Its name need not match the part's installed front.|

## Chapter2 — PDF123 / printed2-37 — 15 questions

|Q|Short topic|Answer for worksheet|
|---:|---|---|
|1|Projection divisions|Axonometric,Oblique,Perspective.|
|2|Axonometric types|Isometric,Dimetric,Trimetric: three equal axis scales,two equal scales,or three different scales respectively.|
|3|Oblique front|True. The front face is drawn in true shape/size; depth recedes obliquely.|
|4|Oblique types|Cavalier and Cabinet. Cavalier uses full receding depth; Cabinet uses half depth to reduce apparent distortion.|
|5|Perspective|Projection rays converge at a viewpoint; parallel directions may converge to vanishing points and distant features appear smaller. Example: railway tracks receding toward the horizon.|
|6|One view for uniform parts|True when one view plus dimensions/notes completely defines the part, e.g.a simple turned cylindrical part with diameter and length. Complex geometry still needs additional views.|
|7|Front chosen by description|True. Use the view that best describes general shape; it need not coincide with the assembly's installed front.|
|8|Diameter symbol|True. Prefix the cylindrical diameter withØ; the OCR's0 is a diameter-symbol substitution, not the numeral zero.|
|9|Third-angle symbol|Draw the standard truncated-cone side view plus its aligned concentric-circle end view in third-angle arrangement. The actual source PDF102/2-16 draws the frustum with larger end at RIGHT,smaller end at LEFT,and the concentric-circle end view at LEFT. Include the center axis. This is a required paper diagram,not a text-only closure.|
|10|First-angle symbol|Use the same source frustum orientation,larger end RIGHT and smaller end LEFT,but put its concentric-circle end view at RIGHT for first-angle arrangement. Include the center axis. This is a required paper diagram.|
|11|Angle convention difference|First:object between viewer and plane,Top below/Right left. Third:plane between viewer and object,Top above/Right right.|
|12|US first angle|False under the book's US third-angle convention.|
|13|Section hatches|True. Conventional patterns may indicate material families; they are not a substitute for exact material specification.|
|14|Mechanical dimension endings|True under the source mechanical-drafting convention, with architectural slash/dot exceptions kept separate.|
|15|Break lines example|True. They delimit a removed/omitted portion, e.g.a long shaft shortened to fit the sheet or a local broken-out region exposing an interior feature.|

|4|Cylinder size view|The view where the cylinder appears as a rectangle normally carries its length and diameter, withØ indicating diameter.|

|6|Hidden-line dimensions|True. Prefer a visible or section view; avoid dimensioning to hidden lines when a clearer representation is available.|
|7|Through-hole suffix|THRU.|
|8|Unilateral tolerance|True. The permitted variation lies on only one side of nominal,e.g.+0.02/−0.00 or+0.00/−0.02.|
|9|Angular subdivisions|Minutes and seconds:60minutes per degree,60seconds per minute. Use′ and″ when appropriate.|
|10|External thread suffix|A,e.g.2A.|
|11|Internal thread suffix|B,e.g.2B.|

|6|Default planes|Front,Top,Right.|
|7|Base feature|The first solid-producing feature on which later features depend,e.g.a rectangle extruded into a block.|
|8|Boss/cut/fillet|Boss adds profile-derived volume;Cut removes profile-derived volume;Fillet rounds an existing edge/transition.|
|9|Black sketch|Fully defined in the book's default sketch-color convention.|

|12|Folders only store part files|False. FeatureManager folders organize features/items; document folders may contain multiple file types.|
|13|Symmetric relation|Paired entities have reflected positions/geometry about a construction centerline; moving one preserves symmetry of its partner.|
|14|Angular dimension|A dimension that controls or reports the included angle between suitable lines/entities,or an entity's angle to a reference.|
|15|Draft angle|A taper on walls relative to the pull direction,facilitating mold release. Example:a molded container whose walls narrow toward one end.|
|16|Arc points|Three defining points for the source arc constructions:3-point arc uses two ends and a point on the arc;centerpoint arc uses center,start,end. A tangent-arc interaction additionally derives its initial tangent from existing geometry.|
|17|Multi-body part|One part document contains separate solid bodies,which can be operated on with body/feature scope and later combined or saved separately. It is not automatically an assembly of independently mated components.|
|18|Feature iconsA–F|A Extruded Boss/Base;B Linear Pattern;C Draft;D Shell;E Revolved Boss/Base;F Mirror. Source PDF257 rendered and visually inspected,not identified from OCR symbols.|

## Chapter7 — PDF368 / printed7-60 — 16 questions

|Q|Short topic|Answer for worksheet|
|---:|---|---|
|1|Six feature functions|Sweep extends a profile along a path;Revolved Cut Thin removes a thickness-controlled revolved region;Loft joins ordered profiles into added volume;Rib adds a thickness-controlled reinforcement from a guide sketch;Circular Pattern repeats around an axis;Linear Pattern repeats along one/two directions.|
|2|Suppressed feature|A retained feature excluded from the current rebuild/configuration,with downstream effects handled through its dependencies.|
|3|Why suppress|Compare design alternatives,simplify a configuration or computation,and temporarily exclude a feature without deleting its definition.|
|4|Rib input requirements|A sketch/guide,thickness,and growth direction;also set side placement,end conditions/draft where applicable.|
|5|Pierce|Constrains a sketch point to the intersection of a selected curve with the sketch plane,commonly linking a sweep profile to its path or a loft section to a guide.|
|6|Swept thread|Create a helix/spiral path,draw a thread cross-section on a suitable plane,constrain it to the path withPierce,and sweep-add/cut it. Example:LENSCAP's linked helical swept thread. A helix alone is not a solid thread.|
|7|Linear Pattern procedure|Select a seed,select a directional edge/axis,set spacing/count and optional second direction,preview required instances,and accept. Example:repeated flat-bar holes.|
|8|Convert advantages|Reuses exact existing shape and retains geometric association,avoiding manual remeasurement/redrawing and allowing upstream changes to propagate.|
|9|Sketch symmetry example|Use a centered rectangle or pairs of entities constrained symmetric about a construction centerline;dimension the shared width and offsets.|
|10|Feature symmetry example|Use Mid-plane extrusion about the sketch plane or mirror selected features about a reference plane.|
|11|Guide curve|A curve intersecting/corresponding to section profiles that steers shape between them. Sweeps and lofts can use guide curves;each profile/guide intersection must be consistent.|
|12|Draft feature|Tapers selected existing faces relative to a neutral plane/parting line and pull direction to support removal from a mold.|
|13|Feature draft versus extrusion draft|Extrusion Draft Angle tapers walls while creating that extrusion;separate Draft modifies selected existing faces and provides independent neutral-plane/parting-line scope.|
|14|Pattern difference|Circular uses angular positions around an axis;Linear uses translated spacing along directional references.|
|15|Convert Entities advantages|Accurate reuse,less redundant sketch work and persistent source association. Example:converted perimeter used by an offset or swept cut.|
|16|Loft only first feature|False. A Lofted Base can start a part;Lofted Boss can add to an existing part. Order depends on the intended geometry and dependencies.|

## Chapter8 — PDF412 / printed8-34 — 16 questions

|Q|Short topic|Answer for worksheet|
|---:|---|---|
|1|Part template for assembly|False. Start an assembly from an Assembly template,with its own units/properties. The book creates ASM-IN-ANSI and ASM-MM-ISO.|
|2|Distance versus Coincident|Distance enforces a nonzero signed separation,e.g.2.54mmSwitch/Handle clearance. Coincident makes appropriate references share a location/plane,e.g.a flange seated on a shoulder.|
|3|Assembly/subassembly|An assembly groups part/component occurrences and mates. An assembly inserted inside another assembly is a subassembly;it is the same document type used at a nested level.|
|4|Five modeling practices|Plan the component hierarchy;ground a meaningful reference component;use functional mating geometry;constrain only intended freedoms;check movement/interference. Additional practices:reuse subassemblies,organize names/metadata,avoid redundant mates,and test source-edit propagation.|
|5|Interference test|Run Interference Detection on the required component scope and inspect exact positive overlapping volumes. Example:the source-selectedHousing/Battery check is clear,while the separate all-visible FLASHLIGHT report records two unreviewed overlaps. Bounding-box overlap alone is not exact interference.|
|6|Deform|A shape-modification feature that changes geometry using controls such as point,curve-to-curve or surface-based deformation. It is different from rigid component movement and is not a structural-analysis result.|
|7|Mates and purpose|Geometric relationships between component references that locate/align parts and remove specified degrees of freedom,while retaining intentional movement.|
|8|Six DOF|Three translations alongX,Y,Z and three rotations aboutX,Y,Z for a free rigid occurrence.|
|9|Fixed component|True within its parent assembly frame:a fixed occurrence cannot move relative to that frame. A nested rigid assembly can still move as a whole in a higher-level assembly.|
|10|Section view|An imaginary cutting plane removes the nearer material for visualization so internal features are visible;cut material is typically hatched in a drawing.|
|11|Suppressed feature/component|Suppressed feature is omitted from part rebuild; suppressed component is omitted from active assembly participation/configuration. Example:LensShield's source suppression/restoration versus suppressing an optional assembly component. Hidden only affects display and is not suppression.|
|12|Concentric faces|Typically cylindrical/conical faces or circular references whose axes can be aligned;actual valid selectable geometry determines the mate.|
|13|Standard mate types/help|Book lists Coincident,Parallel,Perpendicular,Tangent,Concentric,Lock,Distance,Angle. For Tangent selection combinations and options,use SOLIDWORKS Help/Mate PropertyManager help.|
|14|Only planes as references|False. Suitable faces,edges,axes,vertices,points and origins can also be used.|
|15|Only faces as references|False. Suitable planes,axes,edges,points,vertices and origins are also valid.|
|16|Exploded view steps|Choose Exploded View,select components and translation/rotation direction,set distances/angles,record ordered explode steps,accept/save,and collapse/replay as needed. Add explode lines and drawing/BOM balloons separately when required.|
