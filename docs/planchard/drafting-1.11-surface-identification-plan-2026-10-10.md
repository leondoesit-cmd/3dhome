The crucial distinctions are G and H. **G is a vertical bevel**, as its top/bottom edges are horizontal in the isometric and do not follow either principal horizontal isometric axis; both Front and Right show its area. It is not merely an ordinary right end face. **H is the broad sloping rib top**, while the I leader terminates inside the front triangular side. The rib extends to the right of the upright in the long-front direction, so H is seen as a diagonal edge in Front and as area in Top/Right. The semicylindrical surface E contributes a visible notch curve in Front, but its face area projects edge-on there; listing E as a Front face area would conflate that curve with the curved surface itself.

Edge-on does not mean no lines appear: the corresponding top/side edges can still form visible outlines. Likewise hidden edges of E may be represented in a detailed Right drawing, but E has no unobscured visible face area there. The table answers the textbook's surface-identification prompt using that distinction; no dimensioned reconstruction or hidden-line geometry is guessed.
