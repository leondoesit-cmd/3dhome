## Paraphrased questions and answers

1. **Cartesian system:** Mutually perpendicular axes locate points by signed distances from the origin; use (x,y) in2D and (x,y,z) in3D. Source printed1-7–1-9/PDF35–37.
2. **Axes intersection:** The origin: (0,0) or (0,0,0). Source1-7/PDF35 and1-9/PDF37.
3. **Right-hand rule:** Right thumb points+X, index finger+Y and remaining fingers toward+Z. For positive rotation, thumb follows the positive axis and curled fingers show rotation. Source1-9/PDF37.
4. **Why freehand sketching matters:** It develops visualization, records ideas, communicates concepts, helps work out details and exposes problems. Source1-13/PDF41.
5. **First versus Third Angle:** Same six principal views, different arrangement. First: Top below Front and Right on the left. Third: Top above Front and Right on the right. Source1-32–1-34/PDF60–62.
6. **First Angle used in United States?** False in the book's convention; it identifies Third Angle for the United States. Source1-32/PDF60. This is a textbook answer, not a claim that exceptions never occur.
7. **Line precedence:** When lines coincide, show the higher-priority type. Book order: visible, hidden, cutting plane, center, phantom, break, dimension, extension/leader, section. Thus visible beats hidden, hidden beats center, cutting plane normally beats center. Source1-23/PDF51.
8. **Hidden beats visible?** False; visible takes precedence. Source1-23/PDF51.
9. **Two axes create four quadrants I–IV?** True; numbered counterclockwise from upper right. Source1-8/PDF36.
10. **Glass Box method:** Place the object inside an imaginary box, project perpendicularly onto six walls, then unfold walls into aligned orthographic views. Source1-32–1-33/PDF60–61.
11. **First and Third give the same six views but different placement?** True. Source1-33–1-34/PDF61–62.
12. **Section lines can identify material?** True in the book; different hatch patterns may indicate material. Source1-21–1-22/PDF49–50.
13. **Mechanical dimension lines terminate in arrowheads?** True as stated by this textbook. Architectural drawings may instead use slashes/dots; the book also mentions a dot to a face. Source1-18/PDF46. Preserve this qualification rather than claim a universal present-day standards rule.
14. **Break lines represent an imaginary cut/expose interior or fit the sheet?** True in the book. Example: a broken-out region reveals an interior bore, or a long shaft is shortened on the sheet with break lines. Source1-20/PDF48. Cutting-plane lines separately indicate a full section's location.
15. **Front chosen to show characteristic features with few hidden lines; other views follow?** True. Choose the most informative orientation and project the other aligned views from it. Source1-35/PDF63.
